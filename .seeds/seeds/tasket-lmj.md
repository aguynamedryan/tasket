# What tasket is

Tasket is a small, visual to-do list. The name means a basket of tasks, and also "we task it" (Ryan, 2026-11-18).

## Origin

On 2026-11-18 Ryan kept his to-do list in a Google Sheet. Column A was a project name. The cells to the right of it were things to do for that project. The sheet had 27 project rows. 13 rows had no items. The longest row had 5 items.

Ryan's description, verbatim (dictated):

```
down the left-hand side is the name of a project that I work on and then all the
columns to the right are just places where I can put things I gotta do. You know the
nature of my work is that my deadlines are extremely soft. So generally it's just a
matter of me remembering the things I actually need to do, but they're not due at any
point in time. It would help if I could sometimes relate one, you know, relate blockers
between things.
```

## The model (ruled 2026-11-18)

- A row is a project. The items in a row are its to-do items.
- The order of the items in a row is their priority. Ryan reorders them by drag and drop.
- There are NO days. An early proposal had column 1 mean "today" and unfinished items roll over to tomorrow. Ryan ruled it out: "We don't even have a notion of days. just yes, the the order in which they appear is the priority order and I should just be able to drag and drop them relative to each other."
- A finished item leaves the row.
- An item can have a fixed due date. Ryan: a firm deadline "needs to be way more attended to than anything else that I'm doing." So a dated item is the most visible thing on the screen. Fixed dates are rare. The sheet had 2 of them, both due 2026-11-19.
- An item can be blocked by another item, possibly in another project. Ryan's example: the icd9cm item "finish up preliminary extraction" blocks the csc item "incorporate icd9cm". A blocked item becomes unblocked when its blocker is finished.

## Why a list can work here when others failed

Ryan has given up on earlier to-do systems for three reasons: they needed constant grooming, their items were the wrong size, and the list did not drive his work. In tasket, Ryan picks the items himself, at the size he works at. A priority order needs no dates to maintain.
